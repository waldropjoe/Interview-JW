Recommendation

Test what drives late requirement increases

  1. Pull the forecast revision history (census, admissions, acuity) behind each late increase.
  2. Check whether each increase was visible 24–48h earlier. If it was, forecast earlier; if not, hold a buffer.
  3. Meanwhile, flag closures with no spare as “at risk” until shift start. After a rise, they came back 38 of 59 times, vs 9 of 35 with one spare.

PCT follows the same pattern: 16 of 20 returns followed an increase; 14% and 26% of updates rose at 12h and 0h. Limits: shows that the requirement rose, not why · 0h snapshot is a proxy · selected synthetic sample.

Method and data checksDefinitions, how the denominators connect, and every check that passed

Every number on this page comes from analysis.sql, which runs top to bottom in DuckDB and records the expected result under each query.

Definitions used

  • Group: one unit / shift / role, with 7 snapshots at 72, 60, 48, 36, 24, 12 and 0 hours before shift start.
  • First eligible closure: the earliest snapshot where the previous snapshot had a positive open need and this one has zero. Only the first counts. The zero is the observed closure time; the real change happened sometime in the preceding 12 hours.
  • Return: any later snapshot with a positive open need, through shift start. Rates divide returns by all first closures, including those that never returned.
  • Slack: available minus required at the closure snapshot (0 or 1 in this data).
  • What changed: the return snapshot compared with the closure snapshot. Requirement higher, availability lower, or both.

Checks on the logic

  • The previous-snapshot comparison is ordered by timestamp. An early draft ordered by hours-before-start ascending, which reverses time and would have turned every closure into an opening. It was caught in review and the fix confirmed with a spot-check on one group.
  • Return conditions sit in the join, not the filter, so closures that never returned stay in the denominator.
  • The “what changed” split gives identical results whether the return is compared with the closure snapshot or with the snapshot just before it.
  • Headline counts were reproduced independently in Python from the raw CSVs.
